Creative Writing Guidelines — ScenePilot Style Guide
======================================================

CHARACTER VOICE
---------------
- Each named character must have a consistent voice across all scenes they appear in.
- Avoid having a character use vocabulary or register inconsistent with their established profile.
- Protagonists should have agency; they must be the subject of at least 60% of action verbs in scenes they appear in.
- Antagonists should be motivated; their choices must follow an internal logic even if it is sinister.

PACING
------
- Action scenes: short paragraphs, 1–3 sentences. Momentum is paramount.
- Exposition scenes: up to 5 sentences but must advance plot or deepen character.
- Dialogue scenes: alternate between character voices; avoid three consecutive lines from the same character.
- Each scene should perform exactly one of: reveal information, raise stakes, or change the power dynamic.

DIALOGUE RULES
--------------
- Dialogue should feel natural when read aloud.
- Avoid adverb-heavy dialogue tags: prefer "said" over "exclaimed loudly".
- No character should use a word of more than 3 syllables in a tense or action scene unless it is a proper noun.
- Subtext is preferred over on-the-nose dialogue; characters rarely say exactly what they mean.

SCENE OPENINGS
--------------
- Do not open a scene with the word "It" or "There".
- The first sentence of a scene should anchor the reader in place, time, or action.
- Avoid starting two consecutive scenes with the same grammatical structure.

SCENE ENDINGS (BEFORE CHOICES)
-------------------------------
- The final sentence before the choices should create a micro-cliffhanger or decision moment.
- Avoid ending scenes with characters asleep, unconscious, or in stasis unless it is an ending scene.
- The stakes of each choice should be implied by the scene's closing sentence.

INCLUSIVE LANGUAGE
------------------
- Default to gender-neutral pronouns unless the premise specifies otherwise.
- Avoid culturally specific idioms that may not translate across international audiences.
- Do not use disability or illness as a shorthand for villainy or weakness.

SHOW DON'T TELL
---------------
- Replace "She was afraid" with a physical or behavioural description of fear.
- Replace "The city was dangerous" with a specific detail that implies danger.
- Emotional states should be inferred from action and dialogue, not stated directly.
