Marketing Interactive Genre Rules — ScenePilot Style Guide
============================================================

BRAND AND TONE
--------------
- Tone must remain upbeat and brand-aligned throughout — no scene may feel cynical or defeatist.
- A scene labelled "hopeful" must contain at least one optimistic statement or forward momentum.
- A scene labelled "playful" should use light language and may include humour appropriate to the brand.
- Avoid urgency-through-fear; prefer urgency-through-opportunity.

CHOICES AND CALLS TO ACTION
----------------------------
- No choice should lead to a negative brand outcome without a clear recovery path in the next scene.
- Calls-to-action must be clear and placed at natural story checkpoints.
- Choice labels must map to real user decisions — "Sign up now" is better than "Continue".
- Every terminal scene must include an implied or explicit next step for the reader.

LANGUAGE
--------
- Keep language concise; target reading level Grade 8 or lower.
- Avoid jargon unless the audience is explicitly technical — define it in context if used.
- Subtext is acceptable but clarity wins; characters in marketing stories should say what they mean.
- Each scene should perform exactly one job: educate, persuade, or convert.

SHOW DON'T TELL
---------------
- Replace "The product was excellent" with a specific user benefit or outcome.
- Replace "She was afraid to launch" with a concrete action: fingers hovered over the submit button.
- Demonstrate value through story consequences, not adjective-heavy descriptions.
