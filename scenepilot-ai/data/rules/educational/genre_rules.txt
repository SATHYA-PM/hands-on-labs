Educational Genre Rules — ScenePilot Style Guide
=================================================

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
-------------------
- Every branch must reinforce a learning objective stated in the premise.
- Wrong choices should still lead to learning moments, not dead ends — failure is part of learning.
- Each scene should deliver exactly one concept; do not double-load scenes with two new ideas.
- Correct choices should be rewarded with satisfying cause-and-effect clarity.

LANGUAGE AND TONE
-----------------
- Language must be age-appropriate and clearly defined for the target audience.
- Neutral or hopeful tone only — avoid dark or tense framing that may create anxiety.
- A scene labelled "playful" should use light language and may include humour.
- Define new vocabulary in context, never as a separate glossary entry mid-story.

STRUCTURE
---------
- A scene labelled "hopeful" must contain at least one optimistic statement or moment of mastery.
- Choice labels must be short, clear, and unambiguous — the learner must understand what they are choosing.
- Every non-terminal scene must have exactly 2 choices to prevent cognitive overload.
- Stories should build in difficulty — early scenes are simpler, later scenes are more complex.

SHOW DON'T TELL
---------------
- Demonstrate the concept through the character's experience — show the water cycle through Pip's journey, not a definition.
- Replace abstract statements with concrete actions the learner can visualise.
