Sci-Fi Genre Rules — ScenePilot Style Guide
============================================

WORLD AND TECHNOLOGY
--------------------
- Technical jargon should be used sparingly and explained in context.
- Every technological detail introduced must be consistent across all scenes.
- Avoid deus-ex-machina resolutions in any branch — solutions must use established world rules.
- Science and technology constrain characters; they cannot simply be overridden by willpower.

TONE
----
- Keep tone grounded even in extreme scenarios; avoid melodrama.
- Isolation, scale, and consequence are the emotional registers of sci-fi — use them.
- Replace "She was afraid" with a systems-level reaction: suit pressure alarm, tunnel vision, hands shaking over controls.

CHARACTER AND DECISION
-----------------------
- Character decisions should reflect the constraints of the established world.
- Decisions must have systemic consequences — the world changes when a character acts.
- Antagonists should be motivated; their choices must follow an internal logic even if it is sinister.

DIALOGUE
--------
- Dialogue should be efficient — characters in sci-fi settings speak in mission-critical terms.
- Avoid exposition dumps disguised as dialogue; characters should not explain things to each other that both already know.
- Subtext is preferred over on-the-nose dialogue; characters rarely say exactly what they mean.
