Fantasy Genre Rules — ScenePilot Style Guide
=============================================

WORLD-BUILDING
--------------
- World-building details should be woven into action, not dumped in exposition.
- Introduce one new world element per scene maximum; more overwhelms the reader.
- Magic systems must follow consistent internal rules; do not introduce new powers late.
- Physical laws of the world must remain consistent across all branches.

TONE AND ARC
------------
- Tone may shift from hopeful to dark but must do so gradually — never a single-scene reversal.
- Hope is the emotional anchor of fantasy; even the darkest branch must carry a thread of it.
- Sacrifice scenes must feel earned — the reader must know what is being lost.

CHARACTER VOICE
---------------
- Character voice must be consistent across all branches of the same character.
- Each named character must have a consistent voice across all scenes they appear in.
- Heroes face impossible choices, not easy ones — both options must cost something.

SHOW DON'T TELL
---------------
- Describe magic through physical sensation — warmth, weight, light — not abstract capability.
- Replace "She was afraid" with a physical response anchored in the world's sensory palette.
- The world's beauty and danger should be felt, not catalogued.
