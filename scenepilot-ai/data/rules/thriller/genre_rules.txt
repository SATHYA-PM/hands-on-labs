Thriller Genre Rules — ScenePilot Style Guide
==============================================

TENSION AND PACING
------------------
- Thriller - Scenes must maintain tension through short, punchy sentences.
- Every scene must end with an unresolved threat, question, or consequence that compels the next choice.
- Never let a scene settle — comfort in a thriller signals incoming danger.
- Action beats: one sentence per action. Never group two violent actions into the same sentence.

DIALOGUE
--------
- Dialogue should feel clipped and urgent; avoid long monologues.
- Characters in danger speak in fragments. Complete sentences signal false safety.
- Subtext is essential; characters lie, deflect, and withhold — read between the lines.

TONE
----
- Darkness is appropriate; gratuitous gore is not — imply rather than describe.
- Every choice must feel dangerous. Even the "safe" option should carry a hidden cost.
- Avoid passive voice in action sequences; every verb should carry urgency.

ANTAGONIST
----------
- The antagonist must be present or implied in at least every third scene.
- Antagonist motivation must be coherent — fear, greed, ideology — never random evil.
- Antagonists should be motivated; their choices must follow an internal logic even if it is sinister.

SHOW DON'T TELL
---------------
- Replace "She was afraid" with a physical or behavioural description: hands shook, breath stopped.
- Replace "The city was dangerous" with a specific sensory detail that implies danger.
- Dread should be shown through environment: flickering lights, silence where sound should be.
